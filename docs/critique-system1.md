# Critique — System 1 (scene & lighting)

**Verdict: FAIL.** It reads as a clean game-engine scene, not camera footage. The biggest tells are the sky and light disagreeing, a flat orange grade with no shadow contrast, chocolate-brown plastic-looking wood, and tiled ground.

Shots (`shots/system1/critic/`): `default.png` (1600x900), `default_5s_stats.png` (+5 s), `rail.png`, `rail_crop.png` (100 % crop), `default_idebrowser_t0.png` / `_t5.png` (with the stats overlay).
Perf: 200 fps, 5.0 ms, 34 draw calls, 245k tris. Console: only a favicon 404 and a D3D `isnan` shader warning. No perf problems.

## Findings, ordered by impact

1. **The sky contradicts the light.** The HDRI shows a grey overcast storm sky with a rain shaft. The sun is only a soft pink blob behind cloud, yet the ground has hard direct sunlight. The reference (`wooden_fence_sunset_lowsun`) shows a clear or broken sky that is saturated orange-gold near the sun and at the horizon and fades to darker, bluer tones higher up. *Fix:* use a clear golden-hour HDRI with the sun 5–10° above the horizon (e.g. Poly Haven `kloofendal_48d_partly_cloudy`, `golden_bay`, `qwantani_sunset`). Put the DirectionalLight exactly on the HDRI sun direction. Drop the power-line and pylon HDRI, because 2D wires with no parallax look fake.
2. **Flat, milky orange grade with no contrast.** The whole frame has one sepia cast. Blacks are lifted to about 15–20 %, there are no true darks, and highlights are dull. The real shots have deep, near-black shadows and backlit silhouettes, with warm light only where the sun hits. *Fix:* cut fog/haze density 3–5×. Set HDRI env intensity to about 0.3–0.5 and sun intensity to 4–6 with AgX "punchy" or ACES at exposure 0.9. Add an S-curve or LUT that pulls the toe to near 0. Keep the warm tint for lit areas only and make shadows neutral or slightly cool.
3. **Shadows are too light and too warm, and contact shadows are missing.** Fence shadows on the dirt are only about 30 % darker and brown. Post bases look like they float, with no darkening where they meet the ground. In the reference, raking-light shadows are dense and slightly blue (lit only by skylight) and sharp near the contact point. *Fix:* use a bluish, lower hemisphere fill (`#8fa6c8`, about 0.2). Add SSAO/GTAO (N8AO) at radius 0.3–0.5 m, or baked AO decals at post bases. Use PCSS or a contact-hardening shadow (sharp near the occluder, softer further away) instead of uniform PCF blur. Use `shadow.radius` about 2 with a 2048 shadow map fit tightly to the fence.
4. **The wood is the wrong material.** It is uniform dark chocolate/espresso brown and reads as stained or painted, with a faint plastic sheen on the lower rail. The `clear_bottles_on_weathered_plank` reference shows silver-grey sun-bleached wood with warm brown only deep in the grain. It has open end-checks, long splits, raised grain ridges, nail heads with rust streaks and rounded, worn edges. *Fix:* use an albedo of about `#8a8076` to `#a39a8c` with per-board hue and value variation of ±10 %. Use a real weathered-plank PBR set (Poly Haven `weathered_planks` or `old_planks_02`) at 2k, with grain running along each board. Scale it so the grain spacing is 3–8 mm. Use roughness 0.85–0.95 with no specular sheen. Add a normal/height map with strength for splits, plus 2–4 cut-in split decals per board.
5. **The fence geometry is too perfect.** Rails are dead straight, level and parallel. Posts are evenly spaced, perfectly vertical, have identical square sections and razor-sharp 90° edges. *Fix:* give rails a 1–2 cm sag and 0.5–1.5° random tilt, and posts 1–3° lean and ±5 cm height jitter. Bevel edges by 5–10 mm with rounded wear. Add a split or broken end on one rail. Make post tops uneven or chamfered, and add 2 nails per joint.
6. **Composition gives it away as CG.** The camera is perfectly level and perpendicular to a symmetric fence centered in frame. *Fix:* yaw 8–15° so the fence recedes diagonally (as in both reference photos). Add 0.3–0.5° roll and subtle handheld micro-motion (noise of about 0.05° at roughly 0.3 Hz). Use a vertical FOV of about 40–45°, which looks like a 35–40 mm lens.
7. **The ground is tiled and flat.** The dirt shows a repeating pebble pattern, and the plane has no relief. The far field around the horizon is a smooth, uniform plane. *Fix:* sample the texture with stochastic/hex tiling, or mix two scales (0.5 m and 7 m) with a macro-variation noise mask. Add vertex or parallax displacement of 2–5 cm, tire ruts or footpaths, scattered pebbles and twigs, and wood debris near the fence. Fade into the HDRI with a GroundedSkybox or a height-blended horizon band.
8. **Grass reads as cards.** A uniform band of saturated orange grass runs behind the fence, with a hard edge where it meets bare dirt. In front, evenly spaced identical clumps sit on bare dirt. The grass is lit evenly, with no self-shadowing or translucency. *Fix:* use 2–3 clump variants with random scale (0.5–1.5×) and density from a noise mask, clustered against posts and edges. Use a desaturated straw palette (`#b8a27a`) with a darker, green-grey base. Add a backlight translucency term and receive the shadow map. Use alpha-to-coverage with MSAA to stop blade shimmer.
9. **Dust looks like stars or noise.** The motes are uniform pinpoints spread evenly across the sky, the shadowed ground and the front-lit areas. Real backlit dust appears only in the sun-facing cone as soft, out-of-focus discs of varying size. *Fix:* weight brightness with a Henyey-Greenstein phase at g = 0.7–0.85 and multiply by shadow-map visibility. Scale sprite size with circle-of-confusion so near motes become 6–20 px faint discs. Cut visible count to about 200–400. Add faint volumetric sun shafts through the fence gaps (screen-space god rays from the sun position, low weight).
10. **Lens qualities are missing.** There is no shallow DOF (the background trees are about as sharp as the fence). There is no veiling glare or bloom halo from the sun, no grain and no chromatic aberration. The sunset reference has heavy bokeh and glare. *Fix:* add bokeh DOF focused at the rail (focus about 6.5 m, f/2.8 equivalent) so the background is soft. Add bloom (threshold 1.0, strength 0.3–0.5, large radius) plus a subtle anamorphic or ghost flare. Add film grain at 3–4 % luma noise and 1–1.5 px lateral CA at the edges. Add a mild vignette of about 0.25.
11. **The background lacks depth.** HDRI trees and pylons sit at one depth and are fairly crisp, and the horizon line is hard against the flat ground. *Fix:* increase aerial perspective with distance, so far objects lose 40–60 % contrast and shift warm-grey. Add backgroundBlurriness of 0.03–0.05 or rely on DOF. Add a few low-poly 3D shrubs or posts at 15–40 m for real parallax.
12. **The rail top and sun-facing faces lack grazing highlights.** At a 10° backlight, top surfaces should pick up a bright warm sheen and the fence should get rim light. Currently the rails are mostly a flat mid-dark tone. *Fix:* use a Principled sheen or a raised Fresnel F0 of about 0.04 with roughness around 0.7 on the top faces. Check that the sun really is behind-left, and add a rim term for silhouettes against the bright sky.

## Not verified
Grass sway and dust drift could not be judged reliably. The Chrome tab was backgrounded, which paused requestAnimationFrame, and the IDE browser has a different aspect ratio. Dust positions did change between frames. Check alpha-tested grass for shimmering in motion once MSAA or alpha-to-coverage is in place.
